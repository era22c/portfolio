Biofabricated Tissue Models

Rheologically tuned in vitro tissue models for evaluating early-stage muscle repair.

I built a biofabricated in vitro muscle model to study how the early regenerative environment shapes myoblast behavior after injury. The project focused on a translational challenge: many models miss the mechanical and biochemical cues that define early muscle repair.

I designed and optimized rheologically tuned alginate hydrogels, evaluated how conditioned media altered myogenic progression, and used quantitative imaging and data analysis to assess viability and structural outcomes. I also worked with bioprinting and rheology instrumentation, connecting material properties to cell behavior through iterative experimental design.

This Honors thesis was my strongest evidence of independent research capability. It brought together hydrogel design, 3D cell culture, imaging, and process optimization in one platform for studying tissue repair.
